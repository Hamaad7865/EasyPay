    QR ordering Premium A QR code on every table. Guests order from their own phone.

    • A card for each table

      Print the cards from the back office. Each code knows its table, so the order arrives where the guests are sitting.

    • No app, no account

      Guests scan with their phone's camera and your menu opens: search, the choices of a dish, a note for the kitchen. A dish you have marked sold out cannot be ordered.

    • Your cashier says yes

      The order is on the till within seconds. Accept it and it joins the table's order, and the guest's phone says Confirmed. Decline it and the guest is asked to see a member of staff.

    • Nothing is paid on the phone

      The bill is settled at the table as usual. Ordering from the table needs the tablet online; everything else on this page carries on without a connection.

    Front of house See the whole room. Know what every table owes.

    • Your floor plan, as you drew it

      Lay out each room in the back office: main room, terrace, bar. The tablet shows the same plan, with a count of the tables taken in each.

    • A table says where it stands

      Free, seated, waiting for its bill or reserved. A seated table carries what it owes and how long the guests have been there, and its chairs light up for the covers.

    • Bookings land on the same plan Premium

      Take a booking, give it a table, and seat the party when they arrive. The order opens with the right number of covers.

    Kitchen and bar Send once. The right printer gets the right ticket.

    • Food to the kitchen, drinks to the bar

      Each category prints where it is made. Options and kitchen notes travel with the line, so nobody walks back to ask.

    • A clock on every ticket Premium

      The till's kitchen screen lists every send. A ticket turns amber after 8 minutes and red after 15, or the minutes your kitchen sets. Tap a line when it is done, bump the ticket when it reaches the pass, and recall one bumped by mistake.

    • A silent printer never holds an order

      The order still goes through. The till names the printer that did not answer, and the ticket can be printed again once it is back.

    Payment Split it any way the table asks. It still adds up to one bill.

    • Equally, by seat or line by line

      Divide the amount into equal shares, each paid its own way, or move lines between separate checks. The shares are kept until they add up to the bill.

    • Cash with the change worked out

      A keypad, round amounts and the change to give. The service charge you set in the back office sits on the bill as its own line.

    • A receipt the guest can keep

      Print it on 58 mm or 80 mm paper with your logo and your own header and footer, or share it from the tablet by e-mail or WhatsApp.

    • Refunds without the guesswork

      Refund a whole receipt or only some of its lines, with a reason and how the money goes back. Reprint any receipt exactly as it was first printed.

    Selling Scan it, tap it, sell it. The sale builds itself.

    • A barcode or a tile

      A scanner that types like a keyboard rings a product up by its barcode, and every product has a tile to tap. The same product again adds one more to its line.

    • Sizes, colours and weight

      A product that comes in sizes or colours asks which one, and each choice says what is left. One sold by weight asks for the kilos.

    • A discount, or another price

      Ten or twenty percent off a line in one tap, your own figure, or a discount on the whole sale. A changed price holds for this sale only, and can ask for a manager.

    • Serve the next customer first

      Park the sale as it is and start another. Parked sales wait behind one key until their customer is back at the counter.

    Stock Know what is on the shelf. Without walking to it.

    • Every tile says what is left

      Amber when few are left, red when it is out. A sale takes from stock by itself, so there is nothing to keep count of at the till.

    • A stock check at the counter

      Scan a product or type its name: the till shows what the shop holds of each size and colour, with its price.

    • Deliveries, counts and corrections

      In the back office: purchase orders to your suppliers, deliveries received against them, stock counts, and a movement written for every change.

    • Your whole catalog in one go

      Bring your products in from a spreadsheet, with their sizes, colours and barcodes, and print barcode labels for the ones that have none.

    Returns Scan the receipt. The sale opens, ready to put right.

    • The receipt finds itself

      Every receipt prints a barcode at its foot. Scan it and the sale opens. Without the paper, find it by its number: the last 30 days are there, from every till in the shop.

    • Refund what comes back

      Take off what the customer keeps, pick how the money goes back and give a reason. The goods go back into stock, or are written off as damaged when they are faulty.

    • An exchange in one sale

      What comes back pays for what the customer takes instead, and only the difference is paid or given back.

    • Paid the wrong way?

      Change how a receipt was paid without touching its amount. Who corrected it, and when, is kept.
