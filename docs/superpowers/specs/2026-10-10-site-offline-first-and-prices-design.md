# The public site: offline first, prices shown, laid out after another POS's page

Asked by the owner on 2026-10-10, after a look at another restaurant POS's
site: "Lets make our website easypaypos like them but keep our
dotted background and navbar. And then lets lead with offline sales, lets
anounce 5000rs basic starter pack and premium plan starts as from 2100rs".

## Decided by the owner (by tap)

- **What each price buys: today's product.** Starter pack, Rs 5,000, once, per
  tablet: the till, the back office, reports, printed kitchen tickets. Premium,
  from Rs 2,100 a month, per restaurant: adds the kitchen screen and Bookings.
  Premium is a restaurant's only, so a shop's page shows the Starter pack
  alone. They were told this promises the back office with the Rs 5,000, which
  the hybrid plan (`2026-10-10-hybrid-offline-premium-design.md`) later moves
  to Premium; the page changes when that is built.
- **The page plan below**, with our colours, dots, bar and the Restaurant /
  Shop switch kept.

## What "like them" means here

Their page's order and layout, not its words or look: a centred hero with two
buttons over a product shot, a row of three reasons each with a small drawn
screen, one dark band, a grid for payments, printers and devices, questions,
and a table of what is included with the price at its foot. Nothing of theirs
is copied, they are not named, and the page does not call itself the best.

## The page, top to bottom

1. **Bar**: as it is, plus "Pricing".
2. **Hero**, centred: "The till that keeps selling when the internet drops."
   Book a demo, WhatsApp us (the number in `js/config.js`), and one line with
   the prices that goes to the pricing table. The tablet and its film below.
3. **Why EasyPay**, three cards: it sells without the internet; the price is
   on the page; set up with you, in Mauritius.
4. **When the line drops**, the dark band: what carries on (orders or sales,
   payments, printing, opening and closing the day) and what waits for the
   connection (sales reaching the back office, changes to the menu, prices and
   staff, setting up a new tablet), beside a short log of an evening without
   it.
5. **The feature sections, "Also on the till" and the back office**, as they
   were. The offline card of "Also on the till" gives its place to the till's
   updates, since offline now has a section. Bookings and the kitchen screen
   are marked Premium where they are described.
6. **Around the till**: payments, printers, devices, figures for the
   accountant.
7. **Getting started**, as it was.
8. **Questions**, with "How much does it cost?" first.
9. **Pricing**: the table, for a restaurant (two columns) or a shop (one).
10. **Book a demo** and the foot.

## Said with care

- Offline: the till keeps selling, taking payments, printing and closing the
  day, and sends everything on later. Not "no internet needed": setting up a
  tablet, changing the menu from it and the back office need a connection.
- The prices are written as the owner gave them. No annual price, nothing
  about VAT on the price, no hardware in the pack; the page says the tablet and
  printers are the customer's to choose.
- Premium is written as an upgrade to the Starter pack ("Starter pack, then
  from Rs 2,100 a month" at the foot of its column), since two columns side by
  side would otherwise read as either one or the other. That is read from the
  owner's plan, "if the customer wishes to upgrade to the premium plan", and
  was not confirmed by them: it is the first thing to ask before the page goes
  live.
- "Sales periods" leaves the back office's drawing and words: it became "Day
  closing" on 2026-10-06.
